BTCFi Product & UX Redesign

01

Context

BTCFi entered a rapidly growing Bitcoin DeFi market and reached meaningful early adoption, but its product experience still reflected the complexity of the underlying protocol. The next challenge was not adding more interface elements. It was helping users understand what each screen was for, what action to take, and how to manage lending risk with less Web3 knowledge and fewer interactions.

02

Problem

The existing experience mixed product goals, protocol detail, and transaction steps. Popup- driven flows increased cognitive load, important calls to action competed for attention, and the dashboard did not consistently prioritize the information users needed for risk management.

  • Screen purpose and primary actions were not always immediately clear.
  • Borrowing and earning journeys required too many fragmented interactions.
  • Protocol concepts created unnecessary onboarding cost for less technical users.
  • Design discussion lacked a shared artifact for tracing flows and decisions.
03

My Role

I acted as product owner and established the working model across design, product management, research, and engineering. I framed the product challenge, introduced a structured discovery and modeling process, facilitated design decisions, and kept the redesign connected to what engineers could implement.

04

Decision Making

The team aligned on three product principles. These were used as decision criteria, not as visual themes: every proposed flow or component had to clarify the screen's purpose, improve decision quality, or reduce user effort.

Goal-oriented Give each screen a clear purpose and retain only the functions that support it.
Intuitive Present information and actions so users can make faster, better decisions.
Smart Use product and protocol capabilities to remove avoidable user interactions.

Key decisions included simplifying popup-heavy flows, using cards to make lending options comparable, prioritizing health and risk information on the dashboard, and exploring Smart Executor to combine multi-step protocol actions into a simpler user journey.

05

Execution Strategy

The work followed an evidence-to-delivery loop. Industry research and comparable products informed the analysis; an improved IA flowchart exposed interaction problems; “How might we” questions converted those findings into design choices; wireframes and competing concepts then made the choices testable and ready for engineering discussion.

Outcome

The project produced a clearer information architecture, shared product principles, wireframes, two comparable visual directions, and a staged path toward web and mobile implementation. It also created a repeatable collaboration model in which research, design, and engineering decisions could be traced through one user journey.

91.7% saw an improvement

In a 24-person concept survey, 91.7% of respondents judged their selected redesign direction better than the existing experience. Concept B received the strongest preference signal at 58.3%. These results guided the design direction; they are concept validation, not production usage metrics.

07

Lessons Learned

Product ownership is most useful when it gives different disciplines a common language. Principles made tradeoffs consistent, IA made flows discussable, and early concept testing reduced the cost of disagreement before implementation. For DeFi products, the strongest UX improvements often come from engineering capabilities that remove protocol complexity rather than merely hiding it.
