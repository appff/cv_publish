01

Context

After a period focused on network launch and production reliability, the Network Group needed to move from reactive system improvement toward a deliberate technology portfolio. The opportunity was to use infrastructure capability as the foundation for product differentiation across DeFi, interoperability, wallet experience, and security.

02

Problem

A list of independent projects would not explain why the organization should invest, how capabilities reinforced one another, or what had to happen first. The portfolio included foundational reliability work and market-facing bets with different horizons, so the roadmap had to balance operational needs, capability acquisition, and product leadership.

  • Infrastructure work and product initiatives competed for the same specialist capacity.
  • Cross-chain, account abstraction, and security capabilities had dependency relationships.
  • Short-term launches needed to support, rather than displace, long-term technical advantage.
  • The roadmap had to communicate strategy to both engineers and organizational stakeholders.
03

My Role

I created the annual technical roadmap and the narrative behind it. That meant defining strategic priorities, grouping initiatives by capability, establishing sequencing logic, and connecting engineering investments to product and market outcomes. The deliverable was an organizational direction, not a project calendar.

04

Decision Making

I organized the roadmap into four strategic phases. The phases explained the logic of investment: strengthen the base, acquire differentiating capabilities, combine those capabilities into advanced products, and then use the resulting platform to sustain market leadership.

Strengthen the foundation Reliability, security, runtime improvements, and existing-system upgrades.
Acquire core capability BTCFi, account abstraction, naming, KYC, and stronger network operations.
Compound the portfolio Cross-chain messaging, smart wallets, security systems, and integrated DApps.
05

Execution Strategy

Each portfolio theme was expressed as an evolution of capability, not only a target date. Account abstraction moved from optimization to modular and multi-chain support; cross-chain work moved from relayer hardening toward general messaging and broader chain coverage; security work moved from fragmented monitors toward a shared engine.

06

Outcome

The roadmap gave the group a shared frame for prioritization across seven major technology themes and made the relationship between foundational work and product delivery visible. It enabled conversations about sequencing, capacity, and tradeoffs at portfolio level instead of treating every initiative as an isolated request.

Portfolio alignment

The roadmap became the shared strategic baseline for investment and sequencing decisions. Priorities and delivery timing were reviewed against engineering capacity and product conditions as the year progressed.

07

Lessons Learned

A useful technical roadmap explains capability progression and strategic intent. Dates matter, but they are downstream of sharper questions: which capability unlocks multiple products, what foundation reduces future delivery risk, and where can one engineering investment create leverage across the portfolio?
